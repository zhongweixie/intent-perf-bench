One possible contributor is count and offset preparation. Source-major counts go through prefix sums, per-expert totals, minimum padding, alignment and integer conversion before the index launch, followed by device normalization of the returned metadata. These are small tensors, but the sequence of dependent device operations could introduce launch or scheduling overhead that repeats in both forward calls. Whether the device conversions are no-ops and which dependencies reach the activation work depends on the complete path, including count layout and empty-expert handling.

Another possibility is temporary-storage lifetime across the no-grad forward, differentiable forward and backward. Views and saved tensors can retain backing storage beyond the apparent lifetime of a local variable, and repeated allocation of differently padded buffers could interact with allocator reuse and autograd bookkeeping. This could make whole-call latency sensitive to the ownership and lifetime of buffers, even when an individual indexing operation looks inexpensive. The graph's actual saved state and the storage relationships would determine whether this explanation applies.
