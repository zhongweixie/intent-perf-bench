One unconfirmed explanation is the regrouping of received rows into local-expert order and its reverse. Source-major counts become aligned expert-major groups; a sentinel row, padded indexing, and the reverse indexed assignment all touch the activation data, and autograd may turn an apparently simple indexing operation into a more expensive accumulation. Empty experts, padding slots, noncontiguous inputs, and the forward/backward pair make the cost and safety of replacing this path difficult to judge from one operation alone. A more direct row-movement implementation might reduce the complete call's latency. This is a hypothesis; overall measured latency and correctness determine success.
